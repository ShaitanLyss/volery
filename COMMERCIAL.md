Volery is released under the GNU Affero General Public License, version 3 or later. That
licence lets anyone use, study, change and share Volery, on one condition: a version you
distribute, or one you offer to other people over a network, carries its source under the same
licence.

If that does not fit what you are building, a commercial licence is available. It removes the
AGPL's source-sharing obligations and is the right one for:

- a private application you distribute to users, or offer to them over a network, that includes
  Volery or a modified copy of it;
- a commercial product that bundles or embeds Volery;
- a hosted service that lets other people use Volery.

Running Volery yourself, for your own work, needs no commercial licence. The AGPL allows it,
and so does this offer: the licence is about reusing Volery in something you ship, not about
using the app.

If your project is open source under an OSI-approved licence, you can reuse Volery under the
AGPL at no cost. That includes forking it, provided the fork stays under the AGPL.

## Asking for one

Open an issue on the repository's issue tracker with "commercial licence" in the title, and say:

- what the product is;
- whether it is distributed, hosted, or both;
- roughly how many people would use it;
- whether you need to modify Volery or only embed it.

A commercial licence is per organisation and per product. It does not include support, a
service level or a warranty unless the agreement says so.

This page is an offer to talk, not a licence by itself. A commercial licence exists only when
both sides have signed one. Until then, the AGPL in [LICENSE](LICENSE) is the whole of the
permission you have.
